🌊 Fluid density (Biomes) ▼

How does medium density alter behavior?

In the Deep Ocean Biome, fluid density ($\rho$) leaps from standard air density ($1.2\text{ kg/m}^3$) to **$1000\text{ kg/m}^3$**!

According to Archimedes' Principle, the upward buoyant force ($F_b = \rho V g$) increases by over 800 times. Most lightweight objects float immediately. Only objects denser than water (anvils, bowling balls, submarines) will sink.

⚙️ Engine Propulsion & Ballast ▼

Mechanical vehicle physics properties:

● Submarine Ballast Tanks: Submarines achieve neutral buoyancy by pumping water in and out of ballast tanks, adjusting their volume density to match the fluid density. Watch it hover in the ocean biome!

● Jet Engine Thrust: Aircraft accelerate forward by burning fuel and throwing exhaust backwards. The forward propulsion force overcomes drag.

1. Free Fall (Galileo's Law) ▼

In a vacuum (air density = 0), all objects fall with the exact same constant gravitational acceleration, regardless of their weight!

a = F / m = (m * g) / m = g

Drop an anvil and a bowling ball in space to prove they accelerate identically.

2. Terminal Velocity ▼

When dropping objects in normal atmosphere, a friction force called fluid drag ($F_d$) acts in opposition to the velocity vector:

Fd = 0.5 * ρ * v² * Cd * A

As speed ($v$) increases, drag increases until upward drag equals downward gravity ($F_d = F_g$). At this point, net force is zero and velocity remains constant.
